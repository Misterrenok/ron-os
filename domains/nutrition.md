### 2026-09-23 failure-recovery matrix

Morning oat food can be made as a quick one-off if needed; the plan does not require pretending Sunday prep happened.
- **Wednesday top-up fails:** use safe bought/shelf-stable substitutions Thu–Sat as needed and resume at the next batch opportunity rather than sacrificing sleep on Wednesday night.
- **Missing SKU at purchase:** apply the existing nutrient/function substitution logic; exact brand is replaceable unless the substitute materially changes sodium, energy density, storage, meal volume or another controlled variable.
- **Repeated failure rule:** one isolated fallback is normal system resilience. The same failure mode occurring >=2 times in the first cycle is evidence that the architecture, not Ron's willpower, should be redesigned.

### 2026-09-23 taste / real-edibility / cooking audit
Status: **DESIGN RECHECKED / TECHNIQUE CORRECTIONS ADOPTED / REAL TASTE STILL EMPIRICAL**.

The current menu is not a "nutrient spreadsheet that cannot be eaten"; most meals are ordinary workable foods. However, the audit found that **technique**, not ingredients, is the main determinant of whether the plan feels normal or punishing.

**1. Breakfast — oat-flour porridge**
- Main risk: ~80 g fine oat flour + only ~250 ml milk can turn into a dense glue/paste after microwave heating.
- Working method: whisk the dry oat flour with ~250 ml milk **plus ~80–120 ml water to start** before heating; microwave ~60–90 s, stir thoroughly, then another ~60–90 s as needed. Add more water after heating if it thickens while standing.
- Add 5 g flax + 10 g pekmez after cooking. A pinch of iodized salt and/or cinnamon is culinary-only and optional.
- Use a bowl comfortably larger than the wet mixture to reduce boil-over.
- Success criterion: spoonable porridge that can be eaten in ~8–10 min without force-feeding. If still unpleasant twice, coarser rolled oats become the preferred replacement once the owned flour stock is exhausted.

**2. Cold pasta lunch**
- Expected sensory profile is viable: creamy-acidic yogurt/lemon dressing, olive-oil richness, paprika/herb aroma, mild chicken, crisp pepper.
- Main risk is refrigerated pasta/chicken becoming firm and dry.
- Cook pasta fully tender but not mushy; very firm al dente becomes harder when chilled. Drain promptly and spread/portion rather than leaving a hot dense mass.
- Morning dressing for each normal 20-g-oil lunch: existing 50 g yogurt + 20 g olive oil + lemon + paprika + ~1–2 tbsp water, mixed into an emulsion before coating pasta/chicken/pepper. Wednesday uses its existing lower oil allotment, with water adjusted for the same coating texture.
- Chicken should be small-cut/shredded and carry a little retained cooking juice into the portion. Parsley is added fresh.
- Container estimate remains plausible: roughly ~600–650 g finished lunch should fit the known ~1.20-L box with useful headspace, but actual fit/eating comfort is a week-1 observation.

**3. Chicken — critical anti-dryness rule**
- Breast is the biggest taste-risk food in the architecture.
- Prefer uniform 2–3 cm pieces or strips, ideally cut by the seller. Season with curry/paprika/black pepper and normal iodized salt within the weekly ceiling.
- In a nonstick pan/pot, add only a small splash of water, cover, cook at moderate heat, stir/turn enough for even heating, and stop once the **largest/thickest piece reaches 74°C**. Rest covered ~5 min.
- Do not chase browning/crust at the expense of moisture. Retain some clean cooking juice and distribute it with the chicken portions.
- This method intentionally optimizes cold next-day texture rather than restaurant-style searing.

**4. 16:45 work meal**
- This is currently the weakest sensory slot after moving yogurt home.
- Training days: 80 g white bread + 70 g chicken + 50 g tomato + banana. To keep it edible, finely chop/shred the chicken while still moist, store it with a small amount of retained cooking juice, and keep tomato/bread separate until eating. Lemon/paprika/pepper can season the chicken without changing calories.
- Wednesday Etimek and Saturday Nimet variants are inherently drier; fruit/tomato provide moisture, and water should be available. Do not force a dry assembly if it repeatedly fails: two poor exposures trigger a redesign of the moisture carrier/portion placement rather than a willpower solution.
- This slot remains the most likely recipe to require empirical revision.

**5. Lentil-yogurt dinners**
- Best real dish is not a pile of separate ingredients. Reheat lentils until warm, then mix/fold with yogurt after heating; add cumin + dried mint + paprika/black pepper, olive oil at the end. Grated carrot can be mixed in or eaten alongside; tomato/pepper stay fresh for contrast.
- Nimet/bread stays separate for texture.
- This should be moist, spoonable and substantially easier to eat than dry lentils + bread.

**6. Sardine meals**
- Do not mix sardines into the yogurt-lentil bowl.
- Eat the whole Dardanel can as its own component with lemon/tomato and Nimet/Etimek; use the can's oil to moisten bites of bread/rusk. Lentil-yogurt bowl and milk are separate components.
- Sunday ~100 g Etimek is a high-dryness/high-chewing item despite the sardine oil; it is a specific week-1 palatability/eating-time check.
