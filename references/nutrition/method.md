# Nutrition analysis method

Updated: 2026-08-21
Scope: durable reasoning/evidence method only. Current menu/state belongs to `domains/nutrition.md`.

## Data-quality hierarchy

1. Physical product label / manufacturer for reported product-specific fields.
2. High-quality complete composition database donor for unreported fields when category transfer is reasonable.
3. Peer-reviewed or laboratory evidence when the transfer assumption is explicit and decision-relevant.
4. Missing data stay UNKNOWN; never infer physiological zero from a blank database field.

## Analysis dimensions

When decision-relevant, evaluate energy balance, protein amount/distribution/quality, essential fatty acids, vitamins/minerals, fiber/food variety, food matrix and bioavailability, relevant nutrient interactions, preparation/storage, contaminants, hydration, adherence/logistics, price/availability and actual outcome trends. Do not maximize every biomarker/nutrient independently at the expense of the system.

### Total-system ROI / efficiency rule

Optimize the **finished edible outcome**, not the shelf sticker. Compare candidates across the full recurring lifecycle: purchase price; edible yield after bone/skin/trim/cooking loss; active and elapsed prep time; cooking energy/water; storage/freezer burden; transport/trip friction; cleanup; spoilage/failure risk; portioning difficulty; convenience at home/work; taste/adherence; nutrition/spec fit; and how often the work repeats.

For comparisons such as bone-in vs boneless meat, convert both to an effective cost per usable cooked portion and include the recurring labor/cleanup burden. A somewhat higher sticker price can be the higher-ROI choice when it materially reduces trimming, waste, handling, cooking complexity or repeated minutes. Do not chase small nominal savings that create disproportionate time cost, extra stops, mess, or execution friction.

Use dominance first: if one option is at least as good on nutrition/spec and clearly better on total cost/time/friction, prefer it. When trade-offs remain, show the marginal money saved versus marginal minutes/effort added so the decision is based on total system efficiency rather than price alone.


### Whole-system ideal-nutrition scope

The optimization target is **Ron’s total nutrition system and its real outcomes**, not any isolated food, meal, macro target, price, or preparation workflow. Evaluate every materially decision-relevant factor across appropriate **acute, daily, weekly, chronic and lifetime horizons**, and stop expanding only when an additional factor is unlikely to change the decision, safety, adherence or expected outcome.

At minimum, consider when relevant:

- **Health benefit and harm:** energy balance; body composition; cardiometabolic, gastrointestinal, dental/oral, bone, renal/hepatic and other relevant long-term risk pathways; deficiency and excess risk; food-specific benefits/harms; allergies/intolerances; and clinically relevant individual constraints.
- **Performance and recovery:** strength/training output, physical-work fueling, glycogen/carbohydrate availability, protein quality/distribution, hydration/electrolytes, recovery, fatigue and appetite/satiety.
- **Brain, mood and sleep:** acute and repeated effects of caffeine, theanine and other bioactives; alertness, attention, mood, stress response and sleep timing/quality. Separate well-supported acute effects from observational or uncertain long-term associations.
- **Macro- and micronutrient system:** calories, protein/amino acids, carbohydrate/fiber, fat quality/essential fatty acids, vitamins, minerals/trace elements, weekly/episodic nutrients, bioavailability, food matrix and both inadequacy and excessive-intake risk.
- **Interactions and timing:** nutrient-nutrient, food-food, food-supplement and clinically relevant food-medication interactions; absorption enhancers/inhibitors; meal composition/order where materially useful; training/work/sleep timing; cumulative exposure and dose-response instead of assuming that every “healthy” component is better in larger amounts.
- **Food quality and diversity:** minimally processed food share, plant/food diversity, fermentation where useful, fat quality, sodium, free sugar, refined/ultra-processed exposure, fish/species choices, contaminant and preparation risks.
- **Food safety and chemistry over the lifecycle:** purchasing condition, raw handling, cross-contamination, cooking doneness, charring/oxidation where relevant, cooling rate, refrigerator/freezer duration, thawing/reheating, cold-chain transport, container/food-contact suitability and nutrient/quality degradation during storage.
- **Execution economics:** sticker price, edible yield, cooked yield, active/elapsed cooking time, eating time, shopping time, commute/delivery friction, number of stores, energy/water use, cleanup, portioning, storage volume, carrying weight, packaging waste, equipment needs, spoilage and error risk.
- **Schedule fit:** wake/work/commute/training/study/sleep windows, refrigerator/microwave access, realistic eating duration, portability, batch-prep cadence and recovery from missed prep or disrupted days.
- **Adherence and human factors:** taste, texture, temperature, monotony/variety, decision fatigue, hunger/fullness, GI comfort, social practicality, convenience, repeatability and likelihood Ron will still follow the system after weeks and months.
- **Robustness and resilience:** substitutions, seasonal/price/stock changes, emergency meals, travel/work disruptions, freezer/fridge failures and simple fallback rules that preserve most of the nutritional value without redesigning the whole diet.
- **Measurement and feedback:** actual intake rather than planned rows; bodyweight/waist/performance/sleep/GI and other outcome trends; labs/clinical evidence when justified; explicit thresholds for changing calories, foods, supplements or logistics.
- **Evidence quality and uncertainty:** distinguish established effects, plausible but uncertain effects, observational associations and speculative mechanisms. Do not add complexity, cost or restriction for a weakly supported marginal benefit when a simpler intervention dominates on expected value.

For every proposed optimization, ask: **What does this change improve, what can it worsen, over what time horizon, how large and certain is the effect, what does it cost in money/time/effort, how does it interact with the rest of the system, and will Ron actually execute it?** Optimize expected whole-system value rather than any single metric.
