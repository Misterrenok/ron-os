# Ideal Nutrition System Score — 100-point rubric

Updated: 2026-09-05
Status: ACTIVE REFERENCE / CUSTOM PERSONALIZED RUBRIC
Scope: comprehensive practical scoring of nutrition quality for Ron's health, longevity, physical work and strength-training goals.

## Important boundary
This is a custom decision-support rubric, not a clinically validated diagnostic instrument. Its health-pattern backbone is informed by established diet-quality frameworks (HEI/AHEI-style dimensions and WHO healthy-diet principles), while sports-performance, food-safety, logistics and feedback-loop dimensions are added for Ron's real-world use.

Do not present a score from this rubric as a medical diagnosis, disease-risk percentage, or universally validated scientific score.

## Three-output rule
Never collapse these into one number:
1. **Quality score (0–100)** — how good the diet/system is on the evidence actually available.
2. **Execution status** — PLAN / PARTIAL / EXECUTED. Prefilled or planned food is never evidence of actual intake.
3. **Confidence (0–100 or HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW)** — completeness and reliability of labels, diary data, actual intake, measurements and outcome evidence.

Missing data reduce confidence. They do **not** automatically mean poor nutrition and must not be converted into negative quality points unless there is actual evidence of a miss.

## Scoring window
- For a plan: score the designed 7-day pattern and clearly label execution as PLAN.
- For real nutrition: prefer at least 7 adherent days; use 14 days when evaluating energy/weight trend or recurring micronutrient/fish/rotation patterns.
- Judge episodic nutrients/foods by an appropriate weekly average/rotation rather than forcing every day to look identical.

## UNKNOWN / N/A handling
- A subcriterion that cannot yet be observed at the current execution stage (for example real adherence or weight trend before launch) is **N/A**, not zero.
- A genuinely missing or unreliable input is **UNKNOWN**, not a nutritional failure. Lower confidence and identify the missing evidence.
- Confirmed weakness remains in the denominator and loses points normally.
- Calculate the displayed quality score as `earned evaluable points / maximum evaluable points × 100`, while also reporting the excluded UNKNOWN/N/A weight.
- If more than roughly 30 points of the 100-point rubric are UNKNOWN/N/A, or a decision-critical safety/energy issue is unknown, do not present a precise score as settled; label it provisional/UNVERIFIED and prefer a range when appropriate.

This prevents a not-yet-executed plan from being penalized merely because execution-only evidence does not yet exist.

## 100-point structure

### A. Energy and goal alignment — 12 points
- 6: energy intake is appropriate for the current TARGET/LAUNCH goal and activity state.
- 4: observed trend (bodyweight plus waist/belt or other relevant outcome) is moving at the intended rate once enough adherent data exist.
- 2: no evidence of a materially excessive chronic deficit/surplus or low energy availability that conflicts with health/performance.

Do not infer a precise TDEE from a formula alone. A launch calorie target is a calibration hypothesis until outcome trend validates it.

### B. Protein and performance fueling — 12 points
- 5: total protein amount appropriate for body mass, training and goal.
- 2: protein quality and distribution across eating opportunities are sufficient; no need to maximize protein beyond useful range.
- 3: carbohydrate availability supports physical work, training performance and recovery without crowding out diet quality.
- 2: meal placement/timing around work/training/sleep is useful and tolerable.

Timing is intentionally a small subcomponent, not a 10-point standalone category.

### C. Micronutrient adequacy and excess avoidance — 18 points
- 9: vitamins (including A, C, D, E, K and B-group/choline where relevant) are adequate across the normal averaging window.
- 9: minerals/trace nutrients (including calcium, iron, magnesium, potassium, iodine, zinc, selenium and others when data are reliable) are adequate.

Use age/sex-appropriate reference values. Check both inadequacy and excessive intake/UL risk. Low-confidence or missing database fields remain UNKNOWN rather than physiological zero.

### D. Plant foods, fiber and diversity — 14 points
- 4: sufficient fruit and vegetables, with vegetables meaningfully represented.
- 2: legumes/pulses are present regularly.
- 2: whole grains or comparably fiber-rich minimally processed carbohydrate sources are present.
- 2: nuts/seeds or equivalent nutrient-dense plant sources are present when appropriate.
- 2: dietary fiber is adequate and tolerated.
- 2: meaningful plant/food rotation and diversity across the week; fermented foods may contribute but are not mandatory magic items.

Do not award points for a mythical fixed plant-count target. Diversity is graded by meaningful food-group and plant-source variety plus tolerance.

### E. Fat quality and omega-3 — 10 points
- 4: saturated fat is appropriately limited and industrial trans fat is minimized/avoided.
- 2: unsaturated fats meaningfully replace less favorable fats.
- 3: long-chain omega-3 (EPA/DHA) or a validated equivalent strategy is adequately covered across the week.
- 1: total fat remains sufficient for essential fatty acids, fat-soluble vitamins and practical energy needs.

### F. Moderation and long-term cardiometabolic risk — 12 points
- 3: free/added sugars and sugar-sweetened beverages are limited.
- 2: sodium/salt is appropriate for health while accounting for actual heat/sweat context rather than blindly forcing one number.
- 3: refined grains/ultra-processed, energy-dense nutrient-poor foods do not dominate the diet.
- 2: processed meat is minimized and red-meat exposure is appropriate within the total pattern.
- 2: alcohol is absent or low-risk; abstinence receives full credit and alcohol is never required for a higher score.

### G. Hydration and electrolytes — 6 points
- 4: hydration is adequate in real life, judged from intake plus thirst, urine/context, heat/sweat and relevant bodyweight evidence rather than a generic extreme water target.
- 2: electrolyte/sodium strategy is individualized when heat, heavy sweating or long sessions make it relevant; unnecessary routine electrolyte loading gets no bonus.

### H. Food safety and exposure control — 8 points
- 3: storage, cooling, refrigeration/freezing and transport/cold-chain practices are safe.
- 2: cooking temperature, cross-contamination and hygiene are safe.
- 1: relevant contaminant/species/preparation risks are controlled (e.g. fish identity, mold, excessive charring, unsuitable food-contact materials).
- 2: supplements/ergogenic aids, if used, have a justified indication, safe dose, interaction check and credible product quality. No supplement is required to earn full points if food already covers the need.

### I. Execution, adherence and feedback loop — 8 points
- 3: the planned pattern is actually followed often enough to produce the intended result.
- 2: cost, shopping, cooking, transport, cleanup, taste and schedule are sustainable enough for continued adherence.
- 1: GI tolerance, appetite/fullness and sleep interaction are acceptable.
- 2: outcomes are measured with the minimum useful evidence and the plan has explicit adjustment rules rather than endless redesign from single-day noise.

## Total
A+B+C+D+E+F+G+H+I = **100 points**.

## Safety gates / score caps
Point totals cannot override acute or high-severity failures.
- A credible acute food-safety hazard, dangerous supplement dose/interaction, or other material immediate harm blocks a high score until fixed; do not let strengths elsewhere mathematically cancel it.
- A confirmed severe chronic energy mismatch, clinically material nutrient deficiency/toxicity, or repeatedly unsafe execution similarly triggers a cap/hold rather than being hidden inside the average.
- Exact cap depends on severity; state the gate explicitly instead of pretending the arithmetic alone is sufficient.

## What is deliberately NOT a standalone positive category
- **Supplements:** tools to close a need, not a virtue. Zero supplements can be ideal.
- **Meal frequency:** no inherent bonus beyond performance, GI tolerance, adherence and sleep effects.
- **Meal timing:** useful but secondary to total energy/nutrients and overall dietary pattern.
- **Cronometer target completion:** a measurement aid, not nutrition quality by itself.
- **Detox/antioxidant megadoses/collagen/probiotic pills:** no default points.
- **A magical plant-count target:** diversity matters, not an arbitrary number.

## Evidence/measurement policy
- Prefer physical labels/manufacturer data for product-specific nutrients.
- Use complete composition databases for reasonable category transfer; label the transfer assumption.
- Missing nutrient data remain UNKNOWN.
- Planned/prefilled diary entries never prove intake.
- Use repeated/weekly evidence where day-to-day variation is expected.
- Keep quality score and confidence separate so uncertainty cannot masquerade as bad nutrition or false precision.

## Interpretation bands
These bands are operational, not clinical risk categories:
- 95–100: exceptional / no material known weakness
- 90–94: excellent / only small refinements
- 80–89: strong / several meaningful improvements remain
- 70–79: adequate but clearly improvable
- 60–69: multiple material weaknesses
- <60: major gaps or safety/execution problems

A score of 95+ requires high confidence and no open material safety/nutrient/energy/adherence issue. UNKNOWN decision-critical items prevent a 95+ claim even if the arithmetic from known components is high.

## Replaces the earlier provisional rubric
The earlier provisional weighting (meals/macros 25; micronutrients/fiber 20; hydration 10; timing/consistency 10; food quality 15; energy fit 10; supplements 5; practicality/adherence 5) is retired because it:
- double-counted energy/macros;
- overweighted timing and hydration relative to long-term diet quality;
- incorrectly rewarded supplements as a standalone virtue;
- merged fiber/diversity into micronutrients;
- omitted explicit fat quality/omega-3, sodium/free-sugar/refined/UPF/processed-meat moderation, food safety and outcome calibration;
- mixed uncertainty with nutrition quality instead of reporting confidence separately.
