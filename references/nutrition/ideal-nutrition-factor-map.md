# Ideal Nutrition — whole-system factor map

Updated: 2026-09-18 Europe/Istanbul
Status: ACTIVE REFERENCE / ADVERSARIALLY AUDITED PRACTICAL COMPLETENESS MAP — not mathematically exhaustive
Scope: enumerate the materially relevant factor universe for designing Ron's ideal nutrition system. This file is not a mutable menu, price list, diary or execution owner.

## Purpose

"Ideal nutrition" means the best expected whole-system outcome for Ron across health, performance, cognition, sleep, time, money, effort, safety, adherence and real-life execution — not the most theoretically nutrient-dense menu and not the cheapest basket.

The map is deliberately broader than a nutrition score. A factor belongs here if it can materially change:
- health benefit or harm;
- performance/recovery/cognition/sleep;
- money or time cost;
- execution reliability/adherence;
- food safety;
- availability/logistics;
- or the choice between otherwise plausible options.

Do not require Ron to remember or enumerate the factor set. The system owns the checklist and should surface only the decisions/actions Ron actually needs.

## Completeness / stopping rule

The universe of conceivable variables is unbounded. Never call this list literally complete or exhaustive. Treat it as **practically complete for a decision** only after plausible omitted factor classes have been adversarially tested and none is likely to change the selected solution, safety, adherence or expected value enough to justify the added complexity.

### Anti-loop / freeze rule
Do not keep reopening this architecture for wording-level imperfections. A newly found issue is **material** only if fixing it could plausibly change safety, a concrete food/menu choice, procurement/logistics, execution burden, monitoring/adjustment policy, or another consequential decision. Pure semantic refinements, broader caveats, or mathematically more precise wording that would not change action are **non-material** and must not restart design.

Once an adversarial pass finds no material defect, **freeze the current architecture as the working baseline and move to implementation**. Reopen it only when implementation, new evidence, or a real failure reveals a material missing factor or wrong decision rule. Optimization time is itself a cost.

For each material candidate ask:
1. What does it improve?
2. What can it worsen?
3. Over what time horizon?
4. How large and certain is the effect?
5. What does it cost in money, minutes, effort, storage and cognitive load?
6. How does it interact with the rest of the system?
7. Will Ron actually execute it repeatedly?

## Factor universe

### 1. Goal, physiology and horizon
- Current goal: maintenance, slow gain, fat loss, performance, health or mixed objective.
- Body mass/composition trajectory, waist/belt trend and relevant performance outcomes.
- Age/sex/body size/activity/physical-work/training load.
- Acute vs daily vs weekly vs chronic/lifetime effects.
- Adaptation over time: appetite, GI tolerance, training load, season and schedule changes.
- Medical/clinical constraints only when supported by direct evidence; never invent diagnoses.

### 2. Energy system
- Total energy intake vs actual expenditure/outcome trend.
- Chronic surplus/deficit risk and low-energy-availability risk.
- Calorie density, meal volume and satiety.
- Energy distribution around work/training/sleep when useful.
- Calibration rules based on repeated real outcomes, not formula precision.

### 3. Protein / amino-acid system
- Total amount relative to body mass and goal.
- Protein quality/digestibility and indispensable amino acids.
- Distribution across eating opportunities where useful.
- Animal/plant mix, cost, convenience and GI tolerance.
- Avoid paying/time-spending for protein beyond the useful range.

### 4. Carbohydrate / fiber / glycogen system
- Total carbohydrate adequacy for physical work and strength training.
- Timing around demanding work/training.
- Whole-grain/legume/fruit/vegetable share vs refined carbohydrate load.
- Fiber amount, type, ramp rate and tolerance.
- Glycemic response only where it changes a real decision; do not optimize isolated glycemic metrics at the expense of the whole diet.

### 5. Fat / essential-fatty-acid system
- Total fat sufficiency.
- Saturated/trans vs mono/polyunsaturated pattern.
- EPA/DHA and other essential-fatty-acid coverage.
- Oil choice, oxidation/storage and cooking suitability.
- Energy density vs satiety/adherence.

### 6. Vitamins, minerals and trace elements
- Adequacy and excess/UL risk for all material vitamins/minerals.
- Weekly/episodic nutrients rather than forcing identical daily intake.
- Iodine, vitamin D, calcium, iron, magnesium, potassium, zinc, selenium, vitamin E/K, choline and other relevant nutrients.
- Product-label completeness and uncertainty.
- Food-first vs supplement only after need/value/safety review.

### 7. Food matrix, bioavailability and interactions
- Whole-food matrix vs isolated nutrients.
- Absorption enhancers/inhibitors where material.
- Meal/food pairings: e.g. fat-soluble vitamins with fat; iron source/context; tea/coffee timing when relevant to absorption.
- Food-food, food-supplement and clinically relevant food-medication interactions.
- Cooking/soaking/fermentation effects on digestibility and nutrient availability.

### 8. Bioactive compounds / microbiome / antioxidant system / brain / mood / sleep
- Gut microbiota support: fermentable fibers, resistant starch, legumes/whole grains/nuts/seeds, fermented foods, microbial metabolites such as SCFAs, and actual GI tolerance. Do not optimize named taxa or diversity scores as ends in themselves.
- Polyphenols and other plant bioactives: diversity of food sources, plausible gut/cardiometabolic effects, processing/storage/cooking losses, and evidence quality. Do not fabricate a precise total-polyphenol target when product data do not support it.
- Antioxidant system: food-derived vitamin C/E/carotenoids plus polyphenol-rich whole foods, while avoiding ORAC-style pseudo-precision and routine high-dose antioxidant supplementation without a separate indication.
- Caffeine dose, timing, tolerance and sleep effect.
- Tea/coffee polyphenols and other bioactives: distinguish acute evidence from observational long-term associations.
- Alertness, attention, perceived fatigue, mood/stress response.
- Avoid adding complexity for speculative "superfood" mechanisms with trivial expected effect.
- Dose-response and cumulative exposure: "healthy" does not mean unlimited.

### 9. Food-pattern quality and diversity
- Fruit/vegetable amount and variety.
- Legumes, whole grains, nuts/seeds and appropriate fermented foods.
- Fish/seafood/species rotation where useful.
- Minimally processed vs ultra-processed share.
- Free sugar, sodium, refined grain and processed-meat exposure.
- Plant/food diversity across the week without arbitrary magical counts.
- Seasonal variety and locally available foods.

### 10. Organ-system and long-horizon harm/benefit
- Cardiometabolic risk pattern.
- GI health/tolerance, bowel function and clinically meaningful microbiome-related outcomes rather than microbiome metrics alone.
- Bone health.
- Dental/oral health: caries risk from free-sugar/refined-starch frequency and contact time, erosive acid exposure, saliva/dry-mouth context, fluoride/interdental hygiene when relevant, and food texture/stickiness.
- Relevant renal/hepatic considerations only when evidence/health context makes them material.
- Cancer-risk-relevant dietary pattern factors.
- Deficiency/toxicity risk.
- Avoid overclaiming disease prevention from individual foods.

### 11. Hydration and electrolytes
- Total fluid intake from drinks + foods.
- Heat, sweat, work/training duration and thirst/context.
- Sodium/potassium and other electrolyte needs where actually relevant.
- Beverage temperature/portability/access.
- Urination/access constraints only as execution context; do not encourage harmful fluid restriction.

### 12. Timing / circadian / schedule fit
- Wake time, work start/end, commute, university/study, training and sleep.
- Realistic meal windows and time needed to eat.
- Pre-/post-training comfort and performance.
- Late meal impact on sleep/GI comfort.
- Caffeine cutoff.
- Work refrigerator/microwave/water access.
- Batch-prep day/time and backup when prep is missed.

### 13. Eating-time and physical-consumption ergonomics
- Minutes required to eat the planned food volume.
- Chewing burden, dry/hard foods and speed under work conditions.
- Number of containers/utensils.
- One-handed/desk/workshop practicality where relevant.
- Temperature and texture after transport.
- Mess risk, smell and cleanup after eating.
- Portion sizes that are physically comfortable within the available break/window.

### 14. Food safety and exposure control
- Purchase condition, cold chain and expiry.
- Raw meat/fish cross-contamination.
- Safe cooking/doneness.
- Cooling speed and fridge/freezer storage duration.
- Thawing/reheating route.
- Work transport temperature/cold sources.
- Mold/rancidity/spoilage.
- Charring/overheating/oxidation where material, including AGE/acrylamide exposure and repeated oil heating.
- Food-contact/container suitability.
- Fish species/mercury and other contaminant issues where material.
- Hygiene burden created by a candidate food.

### 15. Procurement / availability / Türkiye food environment
- Local availability by home/work/commute rather than national catalog existence.
- Market Fiyatı/local branch stock/price at purchase time.
- Supermarkets: BİM, A101, ŞOK, Migros, CarrefourSA, Tarım Kredi and other local chains.
- Traditional channels: kasap, balıkçı, manav, semt pazarı, fırın, şarküteri, kuruyemişçi, bakliyatçı/aktar where relevant.
- Online grocery/delivery and minimum-order/free-delivery economics.
- Store opening hours, queues, route detour and reliability.
- Seasonality and campaign volatility.
- Shelf/pack size fit and actual edible yield.
- Product-label/spec fit; do not silently substitute because the cheaper item has a similar name.

### 16. Service leverage / externalizing labor
Treat a seller's service as part of the product value because it can remove recurring labor.
- Ask local kasap whether the chosen meat can be trimmed, cubed (kuşbaşı), sliced, thinned, minced, portioned or otherwise prepared; exact availability/fee must be verified per shop.
- Ask balıkçı/fish counter about scaling, gutting, filleting, deboning and portioning; exact service/fee must be verified.
- Supermarket meat/fish counters may offer preparation or cooking services; compare against home labor.
- Current Türkiye example: selected CarrefourSA Hiper/Gurme Lezzet Arası locations allow meat/fish/chicken bought from the relevant counter to be cooked without an extra cooking charge; verify the exact branch before routing.
- Current Türkiye example: Migros Uzman Kasap has dedicated butcher infrastructure and a cooking-service surface; exact branch/service details remain live-variable.
- Prepared/washed/cut vegetables, cooked legumes, frozen vegetables, portioned meats and other convenience products are valid candidates when saved time/cleanup outweighs price/sodium/quality deltas.
- Bakery/şarküteri cutting/slicing/portioning services are candidates where available.
- Restaurant/esnaf/sulu-yemek/tavuk-pilav options can be strategic backup or even a planned meal if total-system ROI beats home prep without materially degrading nutrition.
- Never assume a "free" or customary service exists locally: verify once, then preserve the high-value rule/branch if stable.

### 17. Preparation / cooking engineering
- Active minutes vs elapsed minutes.
- Number of pans/pots/steps.
- Parallelizable steps.
- Batch size vs freshness/texture/safety.
- Appliance choice: microwave, oven, stovetop, pressure cooker, air fryer, rice cooker etc. only if they improve total ROI.
- Water/energy use.
- Ingredient prep burden: washing, peeling, chopping, trimming, deboning, soaking.
- Yield loss from bones/skin/fat/water/cooking.
- Repeatability and error tolerance.
- Seasoning system that adds variety with minimal extra work.
- Cleanup minutes and mess.

### 18. Storage / inventory / transport
- Fridge/freezer/pantry capacity.
- Shelf life before and after opening.
- Freeze-thaw quality.
- Container size, leakproofness and stackability.
- Portioning and labeling burden.
- Cold packs/insulated transport requirements.
- Backpack weight/volume.
- Rotation/FIFO and reorder points.
- Waste/spoilage risk.
- Home vs work storage.
- Electricity/freezer reliance and fallback after a cold-chain failure.

### 19. Economics / total cost of ownership
- Sticker price.
- Price per edible raw gram and per cooked usable portion.
- Bone/skin/trim/cooking loss.
- Delivery fee/minimum order.
- Travel/transport cost.
- Time cost and repeated labor.
- Energy/water use.
- Equipment/container amortization only when material.
- Waste/spoilage.
- Stock-up cash-flow vs consumption-equivalent cost.
- Promotion risk and opportunity cost.
- Do not chase trivial savings that require disproportionate extra stops or minutes.

### 20. Cognitive load / memory / automation
Ideal nutrition must not require Ron to carry the system in working memory.
- One canonical menu/system, not parallel conflicting versions.
- Default weekly basket and default substitutions.
- Reorder thresholds/inventory list.
- One-page "today" instruction: what/when/how much/how to prepare/eat/store.
- Prep checklist with order of operations.
- If-then fallback rules for missed prep, missing product, overtime, training-day change and emergency lunch.
- Fixed storage locations and labeled portions.
- Templates/repeating shopping lists where tools make sense.
- Required food/planning/cooking skills and learning burden; externalize or simplify recurring tasks when skill acquisition has poor ROI.
- Mental effort across planning, acquisition, preparation, storage, transport, consumption and cleanup; convenience is not just elapsed minutes.
- Minimal number of daily decisions.
- Choice architecture: make the healthy/default option the easiest visible/reachable option.
- Reminders only for genuinely useful triggers; avoid notification noise.
- System should remember rules and surface the next action; Ron should mainly execute and report exceptions.

### 21. Adherence / sensory / human factors
- Taste, aroma, texture and temperature.
- Monotony and novelty.
- Hunger/fullness.
- GI comfort.
- Convenience when tired/stressed.
- Social/workplace acceptability and smell/mess.
- Flexibility for spontaneous meals without "breaking" the system.
- Psychological burden of tracking/weighing.
- Sustainability over months, not just one disciplined week.

### 22. Robustness / resilience / fallback design
- Missing SKU/branch stock.
- Price spikes.
- Lost prep day.
- Overtime/late commute.
- Training cancellation/addition.
- No fridge/no microwave/forgotten cold pack.
- Restaurant-only day.
- Illness/GI upset.
- Travel.
- Power/freezer failure.
- Simple fallback meals that preserve most nutrition with low decision load.
- Redundancy where cheap: shelf-stable protein/carbs, backup containers, emergency grocery route.

### 23. Measurement / feedback / adaptation
- Actual intake vs plan.
- Body-weight median/trend and waist/belt where useful.
- Training performance/recovery.
- Energy, hunger, GI tolerance and sleep.
- Food waste and prep/eating time actually observed.
- Grocery spend and emergency-meal spend.
- Labs/clinical evidence when justified.
- Explicit adjustment thresholds and cadence.
- Change one material variable at a time when possible.
- Do not redesign from one noisy day.

### 24. Evidence quality / uncertainty / anti-optimization rules
- Physical label/manufacturer for product-specific facts.
- Current official/live source for prices, stock, law and services.
- Peer-reviewed/authoritative evidence for health claims.
- Distinguish randomized evidence, observational association and mechanism.
- Missing data stay UNKNOWN rather than assumed zero.
- Avoid pseudo-precision and "superfood" thinking.
- Prefer simple interventions with large expected benefit over complex interventions with tiny uncertain benefit.
- Stop searching when the value of more information is lower than the time/complexity cost.

### 25. Information, marketing, social context and supplier trust
- Label readability, ingredient/nutrition information quality and origin/traceability where material.
- Marketing, promotions, loyalty rewards, placement/defaults and app nudges that can distort or improve purchase choices.
- Social norms, coworkers/household/shared meals and eating context when they affect adherence or food choice.
- Vendor/supplier reliability, hygiene reputation and consistency of cut/portion/service.
- Payment/meal-benefit compatibility and recurring-order channels when they change effective cost or friction.
- Food recalls/advisories and regulatory information when safety-relevant.

### 26. Environmental/social sustainability — secondary unless it changes the practical decision
- Food waste and packaging.
- Seasonal/local sourcing where it also improves price/quality.
- Environmental impact of the pattern as a tie-breaker when health/ROI are similar.
- Ethical/cultural preferences only if Ron values them or they affect adherence.

## Türkiye-specific high-ROI discovery checklist

At every major diet redesign or quarterly refresh, test these local leverage channels rather than assuming supermarket packaged food is the only option:

1. **Kasap:** ask what trimming/cutting/mincing/portioning they will do for the same purchase and whether it costs extra.
2. **Balıkçı / fish counter:** ask for cleaning/scaling/filleting/deboning and cooking options.
3. **CarrefourSA Lezzet Arası:** if a relevant branch fits the route, compare "buy at counter + free cook + eat/take away" against home prep.
4. **Migros Uzman Kasap:** compare butcher-counter/prepared cuts/Sanal Market and any branch cooking service against raw-pack home labor.
5. **Prepared vegetables:** compare washed/peeled/cut product premium against saved active minutes and spoilage.
6. **Cooked legumes/canned legumes:** compare dry-soak-cook route vs ready product including sodium, drained yield, active time and cleanup.
7. **Frozen produce:** compare against fresh for price, waste, prep time, nutrient quality and freezer burden.
8. **Semt pazarı/manav:** use for produce when route + price + freshness beat chains; do not create a special trip for tiny savings.
9. **Kuruyemişçi/bakliyatçı:** use for bulk dry goods when unit economics and storage fit.
10. **Fırın/şarküteri:** test slicing/portioning and exact low-salt/whole-grain options where relevant.
11. **Esnaf / sulu yemek / tavuk-pilav / workplace offers:** maintain a small validated backup shortlist by nutrition/price/time rather than improvising while hungry.
12. **Online grocery/delivery:** include delivery threshold, promo, heavy-item transport avoided and time saved in ROI.
13. **Workplace/local merchant deals:** free delivery, esnaf pricing, meal allowance, recurring-order discount or direct WhatsApp order can dominate public app pricing.
14. **Batch-cooking service / meal-prep sellers:** consider only when ingredient transparency, portions, sodium/oil, safety and price are acceptable.

## Current evidence anchors for the Turkey layer

- Türkiye Beslenme Rehberi (TÜBER) 2022, T.C. Sağlık Bakanlığı / Halk Sağlığı Genel Müdürlüğü.
- WHO Healthy Diet, updated 2026: adequacy, balance, moderation, diversity and food safety remain core principles; exact healthy pattern depends on individual needs and locally available foods.
- CarrefourSA Lezzet Arası current 2026 page: selected Hiper/Gurme locations can cook meat/fish/chicken purchased from the respective section without an extra cooking fee.
- CarrefourSA current "Ayıklanmış Sebzeler" category demonstrates a live market for cleaned/prepared vegetables; exact products/prices/stocks are branch-variable.
- Migros current product pages demonstrate ready cooked legumes as an available convenience category; sodium/price/yield must be compared against dry cooking.
- Behavioral evidence supports reducing decision friction with implementation intentions/default/choice-architecture approaches, but the exact intervention should be tested in Ron's real workflow rather than assumed universally effective.

## Relationship to the 100-point score

The 100-point Ideal Nutrition System Score measures outcome quality and execution. This factor map is the broader **design and omission-prevention layer**. A factor does not deserve points merely because it exists; it matters only if it changes the chosen system or the confidence in it.

## Cross-layer regression requirement

The factor map is not a one-time checklist. After a material change to the menu or schedule, re-run only the affected factors **and every downstream execution dependency**. Examples:
- food/amount change -> nutrients + GI/fiber + bioactives/microbiome/oral + dish volume/taste + pack size/open-life + shopping cost;
- cooking-method change -> nutrient/texture + safety/chemistry + active time/cleanup + storage;
- meal-timing change -> training/sleep + transport/holding + appetite/GI comfort;
- batch-size change -> raw-to-cooked yield + fridge/freezer life + container capacity + shopping/reorder;
- product substitution -> label composition + sodium/sugar/fat + price/stock + recipe behavior + Cronometer/reminder text.

A system with stale downstream instructions is **not closed**, even when its nutrient totals are correct.
