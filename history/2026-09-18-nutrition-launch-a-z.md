## 5. Prep architecture

Two main prep sessions remain the default because they balance safety, freshness, freezer burden and weekday labor.

### First launch prep

Budget **90–120 minutes elapsed / about 45–60 minutes active** for the first complete run. The goal is correct workflow, not speed.

Parallel order:
1. Put water on for pasta and eggs.
2. Start chicken prep/cooking.
3. Start lentils.
4. While cooking runs, portion dry bread/nuts and label containers.
5. Finish pasta, chicken, eggs and lentils.
6. Divide into the correct day portions.
7. Cool cooked food rapidly in shallow containers.
8. Refrigerate/freeze the correct portions.
9. Clean raw-chicken surfaces/tools separately.
10. Freeze both cold packs for the morning.

After 2–3 real runs, measured time replaces estimates. Do not assume 60 minutes before it has actually happened.

### Sunday batch: Monday–Wednesday

- dry pasta ~250 g -> 3 equal cooked portions;
- raw chicken ~600 g -> target ~400 g cooked total for three lunches + Mon/Tue sandwiches;
- 4 boiled eggs;
- dry red lentils ~150 g -> about 3 dinner portions;
- portion/label Mon/Tue/Wed;
- keep yogurt sauce separate until assembly.

### Wednesday batch: Thursday–Sunday

- dry pasta ~250 g -> 3 equal portions Thu/Fri/Sat;
- raw chicken ~650 g -> target ~450 g cooked total;
- 4 boiled eggs;
- dry lentils ~150 g -> four portions Thu–Sun; freeze the Sunday portion after cooling and thaw in the fridge Saturday night.

### Chicken safety

Raw chicken is not washed. Keep raw meat separate from ready-to-eat food and clean hands, knife, board and surfaces after raw handling.

The verified safe internal target for poultry is **74°C / 165°F**. A food thermometer is the only reliable way to confirm this. A probe thermometer is therefore a high-ROI safety upgrade, but lack of one does not require abandoning the entire diet: if cooking safety is uncertain, use a fresh safely cooked alternative rather than guessing from color.

## 6. Morning assembly and work transport

Night before:
- containers clean and dry;
- cold packs in freezer;
- chilled food remains in refrigerator;
- dry bread/Etimek/nuts separate.

Morning:
1. Eat breakfast.
2. Take already chilled work portions.
3. Put one fully frozen cold source above and one below/alongside the perishables.
4. Use a compact insulated insert or insulated lunch bag inside/with the backpack when transporting yogurt/chicken/pasta. This is the preferred standard; an ordinary backpack plus cold packs is not a verified equivalent in warm conditions.
5. Go directly to work and put perishable food into the work refrigerator immediately.
6. Keep the cold bag open in the refrigerator when practical so refrigerator air can cool the contents.

If the insulated insert is not yet owned, do not claim the cold chain is verified. The temporary fallback is a fresh bought meal or an ambient-safe meal for the affected slot when transport conditions are doubtful.

## 7. How to assemble/eat

### 12:00 cold pasta
Cut cooked chicken small across the grain. Use pepper thinly sliced or cooked with chicken if raw texture is unpleasant. Mix yogurt with the day's measured oil, lemon, paprika and a little water to make a sauce. Combine only when packing/eating so the meal stays moist instead of greasy/dry.

### 16:45 ordinary sandwich
Mix chicken with yogurt/paprika. Split across two smaller sandwiches if needed. Keep tomato separate and add just before eating. Banana separately.

### Wednesday Etimek dip
Yogurt + finely cut tomato + paprika + dried mint. Keep Etimek dry until eating and dip/spread one piece at a time.

### Lentil dinner
Mash soft lentils with yogurt, day's oil, cumin/paprika and a little water. Eat as a spread with bread; grate/soften carrot if chewing volume is annoying late at night.

### Sardine meals
Open only when eating. Use the can's oil as part of the meal; do not force yourself to drink leftover oil. If oil remains uneaten, log the actual intake rather than pretending the whole oil portion was consumed.

## 9. Failure recovery

- Work fridge unavailable / food warmed excessively: do not save the perishable home portion for later; use a fresh bought meal or ambient-safe backup.
- Prep missed: use a frozen prepared reserve. If no reserve exists, buy a real meal rather than skipping food or cooking deep into sleep time.
- Fish unavailable: Wednesday becomes an ordinary chicken day; Sunday uses another actual labeled fish option or a fresh meal.
- Nimet unavailable: temporary ordinary whole-wheat bread; do not add salt; log the actual bread.
- Grapes expensive/spoiled: another whole seasonal fruit. Exact calories are allowed to move slightly; log the real fruit.
- Lentil GI discomfort: temporarily reduce cooked lentils from 100 g to ~50 g and replace roughly 20 g white bread, then restore gradually if tolerated.
- Missed meal: continue with the next convenient normal meal; do not force all missing food immediately before sleep.
- Extra food: log it; the plan is not allowed to hide it.
- Overtime/training cancellation: food remains nutrition-first; shift timing as needed but do not automatically double or delete calories because of one changed session.

Maintain **two frozen reserve portions** once capacity allows. Refill a reserve on the next prep after use.

## 10. Variety without extra cognitive load

Week 1 stays deliberately repetitive so actual tolerance, prep time, food volume and cold-chain execution can be observed.

From week 2, use controlled rotation without changing the architecture:
- keep total vegetable mass ~300 g/day but rotate some of carrot/tomato/pepper with onion, red cabbage, parsley/rocket/spinach/purslane or seasonal equivalents;
- replace one fruit slot 1–3 times/week with a seasonal whole fruit of similar practical portion;
- rotate paprika/herbs, curry, lemon-mint and yogurt-garlic flavor profiles;
- rotate oily fish when price/spec/availability make sense.

Do not add ten recipes at once. Variety is a controlled substitution layer, not a second menu.

## 11. Hydration, caffeine and sleep

Start with roughly 2 L beverages/day **in addition to 500 ml milk**, then adjust by thirst, heat and sweat. Water is the default. Ordinary training does not automatically require sports drinks.

Caffeine stays in the first half of the day if later intake harms sleep. Do not protect nutrition by shortening the 22:00–06:00 sleep window.

## 12. Micronutrient/supplement boundary

- Vitamin D remains a separate decision. The current menu does not robustly prove 600 IU/day from food. A 600 IU/day D3 option remains a reasonable prevention candidate if no conflicting medical condition/medication/other vitamin D source exists, but it is NOT started by this file.
- K2 is not automatically required with a standard preventive vitamin D dose.
- Iodine remains uncertain because Turkish dairy/fish database fields are incomplete. Do not blindly add 150 mcg/day solely because Cronometer has missing iodine values. Reassess if dairy/fish/iodized salt pattern changes.
- No iron, selenium, B-complex, collagen, detox/antioxidant stack, probiotic capsule or fish-oil supplement is part of the base system.

## 13. Monitoring and adjustment

Execution evidence:
- planned/prefilled foods are not proof of eating;
- actual intake gets corrected in Cronometer;
- only deliberately reviewed days are marked complete.

Weekly:
- count confirmed days;
- weigh under similar gym conditions when available and use a median/trend rather than one point;
- track waist/belt trend;
- note training performance/recovery, energy, hunger, sleep, GI comfort;
- record prep time, emergency meals, food waste and approximate spend;
- note any meal repeatedly eaten through force.

Adjustment rule:
- do not redesign from a single day;
- after about 2 adherent weeks, if weight is flat and the goal is slow gain, add ~100–150 kcal/day at the easiest meal;
- if weight/waist rise too quickly, remove ~100–150 kcal/day;
- change one material variable at a time when possible.
