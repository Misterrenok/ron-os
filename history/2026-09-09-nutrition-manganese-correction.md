# Nutrition manganese correction — 2026-09-09

Status: **AUDIT CORRECTION / MENU UNCHANGED / EXECUTION NOT STARTED**

## Why this exists
The Sep8 ideality audit flagged manganese after a rough donor-based estimate. Ron correctly questioned whether dietary manganese can be measured precisely and whether the toxicity threshold is well established. This note corrects the interpretation and arithmetic without mutating the menu.

## Correct interpretation
- EFSA 2023 could not establish a manganese UL because available human/animal evidence was insufficient to define a reliable dose-response/reference point for dietary manganese neurotoxicity.
- EFSA's adult **8 mg/day safe level is NOT a toxicity threshold or UL**. It was derived conservatively from observed background intakes among high consumers. Adult P95 background intakes across surveyed European populations reached up to ~9.87 mg/day; one vegetarian survey reported P95 ~11.3 mg/day. EFSA stated there was no indication of adverse effects in the general population at background dietary-intake levels.
- The older U.S. FNB/NIH adult UL is **11 mg/day**, derived differently. Neither 8 nor 11 mg should be treated as a precise personal cliff-edge.
- NIH notes that no evidence shows manganese toxicity from high manganese intakes from ordinary food; established toxicity evidence is stronger for occupational inhalation, very high-manganese water and impaired elimination (e.g. chronic liver disease).
- Humans absorb only about 1–5% of dietary manganese; absorption efficiency falls as intake rises, and >90% of absorbed manganese is excreted via bile. Iron status affects absorption.
- There is no validated routine biomarker that converts a normal blood/urine/hair value into an accurate estimate of chronic dietary manganese exposure. EFSA explicitly lists a reliable exposure biomarker as a research gap.

## What can and cannot be measured
- Ordinary practical method: estimate intake from weighed portions + food-composition databases/labels. This is only as good as the product data. Manganese is often absent from branded labels and varies by grain variety, soil, milling/processing and database donor.
- Laboratory-level intake measurement is possible via duplicate-diet sampling (duplicate portions of all consumed food/water analyzed by ICP-MS/AAS), but this is impractical and still measures intake, not individual absorbed brain dose.
- Blood/urine/hair testing is not a reliable routine solution for chronic dietary exposure in an otherwise non-occupational setting.

## Arithmetic correction for the current ordinary-day screen
Using one internally consistent set of USDA-like generic donors:
- oat flour 80 g at 4.019 mg/100 g -> ~3.22 mg
- whole-wheat bread 100 g at 2.174 mg/100 g -> ~2.17 mg
- almonds 30 g at 2.179 mg/100 g -> ~0.65 mg
- walnuts 15 g at 3.414 mg/100 g -> ~0.51 mg
- cooked lentils 100 g at 0.494 mg/100 g -> ~0.49 mg
- white bread 170 g at 0.536 mg/100 g -> ~0.91 mg
Subtotal -> **~7.96 mg/day**, not the earlier ~8.85 mg "before pasta/vegetables" statement.
- generic enriched dry pasta 83.33 g at 0.917 mg/100 g adds ~0.76 mg -> major-source subtotal **~8.72 mg/day** before small contributions from produce, water, spices, etc.

This remains a donor-model estimate, not product-specific truth for İpek oat flour, Nimet, Uno, Piyale or the physical foods. It should not be converted into a diagnosis or precise risk percentage.

## Current decision
- **Do not change the menu solely because the donor-model total may be around/above 8 mg/day.** Natural-food intake in this range is not established as harmful, and the 8 mg EFSA value is a conservative safe level rather than a demonstrated toxicity threshold.
- Do not add manganese supplements or manganese-fortified products without a specific indication.
- Manganese remains a **low-priority uncertainty**, not a launch blocker. Revisit only if substantially higher manganese sources are added (e.g. supplements/fortification, very high tea intake, high-Mn water) or if new human dose-response evidence materially changes the risk assessment.
- This correction downgrades the prior Sep8 manganese concern; the more material unresolved nutrition items remain vitamin D, iodine-data uncertainty, actual adherence/GI/taste, real energy calibration and product/stock verification.
