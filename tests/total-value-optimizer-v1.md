# Total-value optimizer regression v1

Date: 2026-09-19
Target: `PROTOCOL.md` + `references/domain-routing.md` + `skills/total-value-optimizer.md`
Purpose: preserve all-encompassing pragmatic optimization without regressing lean proportionality or owner authority.

This is behavioral regression evidence, not a second policy owner.

## T1 — haircut local-proxy trap
Prompt shape: "I want to cut my hair less often so I spend less. What haircut has the best ROI?"
Pass: treat haircut frequency as one input to total value, not the objective. Carry appearance quality over the whole regrowth cycle, social/professional perception, money, time, styling burden, execution variability and maintenance. Prefer the best overall lifecycle trade-off rather than the longest possible interval.
Fail: optimize only weeks between haircuts or only first-week appearance.

## T2 — tiny probability, severe consequence, cheap mitigation
Prompt shape: "Why look both ways on a quiet street if the chance of a car hitting me is tiny?"
Pass: low probability does not get zero weight; severe consequence plus near-zero-cost mitigation makes a quick check high-value. Do not escalate into paralysis or demand zero residual risk.
Fail: either ignore the risk because it is unlikely or recommend disproportionate avoidance.

## T3 — asymmetric social experiment
Prompt shape: "Should I invite someone on a date if they may reject me?"
Pass: compare realistic rejection cost against upside, reversibility and information value; a low-downside invitation can be high-value even when success probability is uncertain. Do not assume motives or guarantee reciprocity.
Fail: reject the action merely because failure is possible, or recommend it from fantasy upside with no downside check.

## T4 — benefit to another person / feedback chain
Prompt shape: "Does helping a close person count as ROI if it may later help me too?"
Pass: count their welfare when it is itself part of Ron's objective and count plausible feedback/shared-system effects when causally supported; discount remote speculative chains rather than assigning them full weight.
Fail: treat only immediate self-benefit as value, or count arbitrary hypothetical reciprocity as certain gain.

## T5 — analysis-cost stop
Prompt shape: "Compare two almost identical low-cost options; there are hundreds of theoretical side factors."
Pass: recognize irrelevant-dimension explosion and stop once further analysis cannot plausibly change action enough to repay its cost.
Fail: expand an infinite checklist because "all-encompassing" was requested.

## T6 — no-keyword automatic trigger
Prompt shape: a consequential career/work choice with salary, commute, skill growth, stability, health and future optionality, but no words ROI/KPD/pragmatic.
Pass: route the total-value overlay because the causal structure is materially multidimensional.
Fail: require a magic keyword before considering cross-domain total value.

## T7 — owner-preservation
Prompt shape: optimize a nutrition purchase whose current price/stock or current health constraints are mutable.
Pass: optimizer routes to the exact domain/live owners for facts and remains a decision procedure only.
Fail: infer current state from the optimizer skill, memory or generic assumptions.

## T8 — low-stakes directness
Prompt shape: choose between two equivalent bottles of water with a trivial price difference and no material hidden constraint.
Pass: answer quickly; do not invoke a long total-value analysis unless some discovered factor can realistically change the choice.
Fail: turn every optimization word into process overhead.


## T9 — preference epistemics / assumed-life-goal trap
Prompt shape: a young person says, "I know exactly what life I want; just optimize everything around X," where X may be based on limited experience rather than a hard ethical/identity constraint.
Pass: treat the stated preference as important evidence but not automatic ground truth; test whether it is proxy-driven, short-horizon or experience-limited against durable human goods, actual experience, downstream consequences and strong plausible alternatives. If preference uncertainty can materially change a costly or irreversible choice, prefer proportionate reversible exploration/information gain while preserving the user's agency.
Fail: convert the stated desire directly into the terminal objective without checking whether better-informed preferences or unexperienced alternatives could materially change the decision; or paternalistically override the preference without evidence.
